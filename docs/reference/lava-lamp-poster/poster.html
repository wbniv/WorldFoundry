The lava lamp

Two liquids that will not mix, one bulb, and a density that changes with heat: a slow convection engine, and a clock built around it
REVIEW DRAFT · awaiting Will’s visual review · not for print

Invented in 1963 by Edward Craven Walker, whose company became Mathmos; Mathmos lamps have been made in Poole, Dorset, ever since.1

This one is a World Foundry clock: a 1963-style Astro whose wax moves by a small physical model written in Forth, with the time in the holes of its base.

01 The lamp in this clock

The shipped scene in Cycles: 6 blobs posed as on the engine’s first frame, Orange in Violet, 21:47 in the base.

The US Lava Lite Century’s gold base “is perforated with tiny holes which simulate starlight”.3

Ours spell the time, 24-hour, on a 29 × 9 grid; the other holes glow faintly. They change once a minute. The wax never knows the time.

02 How it moves

A bulb heats the bottle. Common wax floats on water at any temperature, so it was weighted to nearly the water’s density. Heated, the wax expands, grows less dense and rises; near the cooler top it gives up heat, grows denser and sinks.1

The lamp’s patent puts the heater beneath the bottle and, at its base, “a surface tension breaker comprising a spiral wire ring”, so blobs coming down merge again.2

Fluid dynamicists file the motion as a form of Rayleigh–Taylor instability. Shake a warm lamp and the two liquids emulsify into a cloudy mess.1

The first time, the wax may need four hours or more to melt through.3

03 What is in the bottle
Paraffin waxabout 0.9 g/cm³; melts at 46–68 °C, by grade5
On meltingits volume grows by up to 10 %6
Carbon tetrachloride1.59 g/cm³, about 60 % denser than water; nonflammable, mixes with wax7,1
The 1968 recipemineral oil, light paraffin, carbon tetrachloride and dye, fluent at “45 50 C.”2
Since 1970no carbon tetrachloride in US lamps (banned for toxicity)1
Todaythe US formula is a trade secret; Mathmos still uses Craven Walker’s, improved with him in the 1990s1,8

The idea, not data: the wax’s density falls faster with heat than the water’s.

04 A short history
1950

Donald Dunnet files GB 703,924. Craven Walker later sees such an egg timer bubbling on a pub stove.4,1

1959

As “Michael Keatering” he directs Travelling Light, the UK’s first naturist film on public release.9

1963

His company, Crestworth, launches the first Astro lava lamp.1

1965

Wertheimer and Spector see it at a German trade show and buy the US rights: the Lava Lite.1

1968

US 3,387,396 is granted to Crestworth.4

1970

Carbon tetrachloride, banned for toxicity, leaves US lamps.1

late 1970s

Larry Haggerty buys the US rights and makes the lamps for over 30 years.1

1992

Crestworth, run by Granger and Mulley since 1989, becomes Mathmos, after the lake in Barbarella.8

1996

Silicon Graphics files Lavarand: random numbers from pictures of lava lamps.10,4

1999

Mathmos sells 800,000 lamps a year, up from 10,000 in 1989.8

2000

Craven Walker dies, aged 82, still a consultant to Mathmos.9

2017

Cloudflare’s San Francisco lobby: a wall of about 100 lamps, a source of randomness.10,11

Copied from the level’s director, which a generator writes from the Python model in 32-bit floats; a test runs both step for step in the real zForth VM. Blob i lives in mailboxes 100 + 10i; a frame runs whole 1/30 s steps, at most 4. Only the line breaks are ours.

Over 20 simulated minutes the 6 blobs spend 31 % of their time pooled on the coil, 29 % resting under the cap and 39 % moving; a cycle takes about 175 s. Game tuning, not a measured lamp.

07 Four ways to move it
✓Forth model: buoyancy, heat and pooling in the level’s script (ships)
✓Native word: the pair loop only, lava-pairs in C++ (ships: cast1 missed frames with it in Forth)
½Baked tape: only the 150 s warm-up, run at build time (so the level opens mid-flow)
–Jolt: rigid spheres pushed by buoyancy (not used: rigid bodies do not change shape)

Shapes are morph targets (rise, fall, squash, neck, two wobbles) that the engine’s Morph deformer blends each frame.
