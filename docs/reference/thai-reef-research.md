# Gulf of Thailand reef: research ledger

Final, 9 November 2026. **Every species in the tank has a Gulf of Thailand record.** The pair of fish is now the reticulated dascyllus *Dascyllus reticulatus* (Will, 2026‑11‑09, decision D10 of the [finishing plan](../plans/2026-11-08-reef-tanks-finish.md)), because no citable Gulf record was found for the humbug *D. aruanus* ([D1 and D2 searches](#d1-and-d2-searches)). This is a source ledger and the final species list; it is **not an approval to print** (the poster is parked, Will, 2026‑11‑09: "park poster"). A published occurrence is evidence of presence at the stated place and time, not proof of abundance at every reef. Genus-only records do not establish a particular species. Behaviour and timing invented for the simulation must be labelled as such.

**The chosen species, *Dascyllus reticulatus*.**

- **Gulf records:** Satapoomin 2000 Gulf reef-fish checklist ([EOL extract](https://zenodo.org/records/19860153)); [Dat et al. 2023](https://bioflux.com.ro/docs/2023.3296-3315.pdf), Table 1, Tho Chu Island; [GBIF 473068089](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/473068089) (California Academy of Sciences specimen, off Koh Samet, Rayong, 1960‑04‑29); 45 iNaturalist research-grade Gulf observations, most at Ko Tao, e.g. [iNat 371192314](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/371192314) (Ko Tao, 2026‑06‑13). - **Coral association, other region:** [Pratchett et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-73979-0) found *D. reticulatus* mostly on *Pocillopora* cf. *damicornis* at Lizard Island (111 of 197 individuals), also on *Stylophora pistillata* and *P. meandrina*, occasionally on *Acropora*. That is the tank's host coral, so the role is unchanged.
- **Appearance:** [FishBase](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Dascyllus-reticulatus.html): body whitish, snout and forehead greenish, a black bar on the front of the body and a fainter one on the rear, blackish scale margins (the "reticulated" net), pelvic fins mostly black, pectorals clear with a spot at the base; colour varies with conditions. [Reef Life Survey](https://reeflifesurvey.com/species/dascyllus-reticulatus/): grey body of varying darkness, a black bar behind the head continuing onto the dorsal and pelvic fins, a second dark bar below the rear dorsal on some individuals; maximum 9 cm.
- **Content rule:** no other tank uses it. `git grep -n -i "dascyllus\|reticulat"` over the tracked tree on 2026‑11‑09 matched only this tank's plan, mockups, scene and poster files, and no other tank's species list.

## Behaviour and husbandry sources read

- **Dascyllus and coral, other region:** [Pratchett et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-73979-0.pdf) report *D. aruanus* on pocilloporid corals, predominantly *Pocillopora cf. damicornis*, and the tank's *D. reticulatus* mostly on the same coral (111 of 197 individuals), at Lizard Island on the Great Barrier Reef. This supports a coral-association claim, but not the simulation's exact paired spacing or 2.5-second dive timer.
- **Guard crabs, other region:** [McKeon and Moore 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4183949/) experimentally studied several *Trapezia* species defending corals against predators in Moorea. Their experiments did not test *T. cymodoce* against a boxfish. [Pratchett 2001](https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/13321/) reports associations involving *T. cymodoce* and crown-of-thorns feeding preferences in the western Pacific. The boxfish threat response and durations are authored game behaviour.
- **Yellow boxfish and a real aquarium:** [Marine Research Centre, Fishes of the Maldives](https://www.mrc.gov.mv/assets/Uploads/fishes-of-the-maldives.pdf), *Ostracion cubicus* entry, states that boxfishes produce a skin toxin when alarmed. This is species-level guidance from the Maldives, not an observation made in the Gulf or an assay of the game fish. A precise toxicity mechanism, risk to other aquarium inhabitants, and safe husbandry guidance need additional specialist sources before printing.

All remain artistic; generated illustrations are not sources for morphology or occurrence.
- [EMECS enclosed coastal seas guide](https://www.emecs.or.jp/upload/publish/encsea_world_guidebook_en.pdf), Gulf of Thailand entry: the Gulf is a semi-enclosed sea and the cited average depth is about 45 m. This is a geographic summary, not the reef depth.
- [Wisespongpand et al. 2024](https://li01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/JFE/article/download/263011/178967/1052230), survey description: their Koh Tao reef survey sites ranged from 5 to 14 m and included rocky and sandy bottoms. This is the sampled sites' range only.
- [Eastern Gulf reef assessment, §4.1.1](https://www.apn-gcr.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/41f9a984933ec9bf0cb1e9b36a1e3e0b.pdf): massive *Porites* dominated almost all of the specific inshore sites surveyed. This cannot be generalized to all Gulf reefs.
- [GCRMN East Asia regional report 2020](https://icriforum.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/GCRMN_East_Asia_Regional_Report_2020-high_resol_spread.pdf), Thailand section: reports severe bleaching in the Gulf in 1998. It does not establish the 2016 or 2024 Gulf-specific claims.
- [Pengsakun et al. 2019, inner-Gulf coral monitoring](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/aos/EN/1245664136378564803): compares bleaching in 2010 and 2011 and finds effects varied by taxon. Avoid implying all reefs or coral types were equally affected.
- [Scott et al. 2017, Koh Tao coral assemblages](https://www.scesap.org/ce_archive/ce2017/scott2017a.pdf), Fig. 2: describes Mango Bay as a fringing reef and other Koh Tao sites as varied reef and rocky habitats. Fringing reefs occur here, but the Gulf is not exclusively fringing reef.
- [Pengsakun et al. 2019, inner-Gulf coral monitoring](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/aos/EN/1245664136378564803), site description: describes turbid coral communities near river mouths dominated by *Porites* and describes monsoon-dependent circulation. This does not prove that all island reefs have the same turbidity.
- **Guard crab diet, other region:** [Shmuel et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.964725/full) observed *T. cymodoce* and *T. digitalis* taking demersal plankton at night in the Red Sea. - **2016 caveat:** [Wisespongpand et al. 2024](https://li01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/JFE/article/download/263011/178967/1052230), p. 169, cites a 2016 agency source describing Koh Tao bleaching from 2010 to 2016 as partial rather than severe; the sentence is not a verified claim of a Gulf-wide mass event in 2016. The originally suggested list of severe events must not be printed as written.
- **Unidentified Gulf ascidian appearance:** [Munkongsomboon and Putchakarn, regional biodiversity report](https://elibrary.tsri.or.th/fullP/RDG4430003/RDG4430003_s01.pdf), §5.1.7, includes a field photograph captioned *Polycarpa* sp. at the southern-Gulf reef survey. It is a morphology lead for a genus-level model, not evidence that the pictured animal is *P. aurata* or any named species.
- **Cleaner shrimp alternative, different genus:** [ONEP crustacean checklist](https://chm-thai.onep.go.th/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/ONEP_V05.pdf), p. 29, explicitly lists *Stenopus hispidus* in Chon Buri, Gulf of Thailand (the table was visually checked). [Vaughan et al. 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-32293-6.pdf) studied parasite reduction with this shrimp under Australian laboratory conditions. This is a different genus from the planned *Lysmata*, so it is an alternative for discussion rather than an automatic congener substitution. Will explicitly approved *S. hispidus* as the different-genus replacement. The older Thai checklist entry for *L. vittata* remains taxonomically ambiguous under the later species-complex study described above.
- **Feather duster withdrawal, other region:** [Museums Victoria, *Sabellastarte*](https://collections.museumsvictoria.com.au/species/8516) describes rapid crown withdrawal when disturbed. It is a genus-level account, not a measurement from Gulf specimens.
- **Clam shadow response, other region:** [Soo and Todd 2014, review of giant clam behaviour](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4231208/) reports studies of *T. squamosa* sensitivity to shadow and touch. The proposed 35% closure is an authored animation, not the paper's measurement.
- **Nudibranch diet, other region:** [van Alphen et al. 2011, feeding strategies at Halmahera](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00338-010-0698-y) reports *P. varicosa* feeding on *Axinyssa* sponge there; it does not identify the diet of Gulf individuals.
- **Hermit crab diet, other region:** [Jia et al. 2024, Nansha Islands gut-content study](https://www.jto.ac.cn/EN/abstract/abstract11363.shtml) reports coralline algae, macroalgae and bryozoans in *D. lagopodes*. Five specimens of that species were sampled, and this is not a Gulf diet survey.
- **Clam nutrition, general biology:** [Soo and Todd 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4231208/) review giant clams' switch to both filter feeding and nutrition from photosynthetic symbionts as they develop. This is a group-level claim, not a quantified *T. squamosa* diet in the Gulf.
- **Orange cup coral feeding, other region:** [University of the West Indies species profile](https://sta.uwi.edu/fst/lifesciences/sites/default/files/lifesciences/documents/ogatt/Tubastraea_coccinea%20-%20Orange%20Cup%20Coral.pdf) reports that *T. coccinea* captures zooplankton with tentacle stinging cells. The profile describes Trinidad and Tobago ecology, where it is an introduced species; it does not measure feeding in the Gulf.
- **Feather duster diet, genus account:** [Smithsonian NEMESIS, *Sabellastarte* sp.](https://invasions.si.edu/nemesis/species_summary/-529) lists suspension feeding on phytoplankton and detritus. The source is a genus-level account; it is not a Gulf individual diet study.
- **Banded coral shrimp morphology, other region:** [Western Australian Museum](https://museum.wa.gov.au/online-collections/names/Stenopus-hispidus) describes red-and-white body and claw bands, enlarged spiny third legs and two pairs of long antennae; the field context is Australia. The poster drawing is schematic and the model still needs species-specific visual review.
- **Feather-star activity, other region:** [Meyer and Macurda 1980](https://micronesica.org/sites/default/files/ecology_and_distrubution_of_the_shallow-water_crinoids_of_palau_and_guam_micronesica_vol._16_no._1_june_1980o.pdf), pp. 85–86, report *L. palmata* hidden under coral heads or in crevices by day, and forming a filtration fan across the current at night in Palau. This is an observation of sheltering, not proof that the animal hides specifically to avoid predators. A [Zoological Survey of India description](https://www.vliz.be/imisdocs/publications/342451.pdf), p. 353, describes 27–41 arms; the design's ten-arm model must be reworked or another documented species selected.
- **Horned sea star feeding and aquarium compatibility:** [Thomassin 1976](https://hmr.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1007/BF01610796.pdf), p. 60, observed *P. nodosus* eating algal films and sediment organisms on Madagascar reef flats; this is not evidence that it eats only those foods. [Gumanao et al. 2023](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/galaxea/25/2/25_G26-6/_pdf/-char/ja) document unusual predation on *L. laevigata* in the Philippines and cite reports of sponge and soft-coral feeding. [OATA aquarium care guidance](https://ornamentalfish.org/what-we-do/advice-information/care-sheets/caresheets-marine-invertebrates/how-to-look-after-starfish-sea-urchins-and-sea-cucumbers/) warns of consumption of sponges and tube worms. Its occurrence near living coral does not establish compatibility with this aquarium's sessile invertebrates; this alternative requires careful design review, not automatic approval.
- **Boulder coral food, other region:** [Sangmanee et al. 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0025326X20305233) tested *Porites lutea* from Okinawa in short incubations and measured consumption of pico- and nanoplankton. This does not quantify the Gulf colony's diet.
- **Flowerpot coral retraction, another species:** [Anderson 1976](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12242/) measured coordinated retraction in *Goniopora lobata* after mechanical or electrical stimulation. The Gulf record is only identified to *Goniopora* genus; the experiments do not establish its species or its actual defense response.
- **Sponge compounds, other region:** [Vietnamese *Xestospongia testudinaria* compound study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23829580/) tested isolated compounds against bacterial adhesion in the laboratory. This does not establish a defense against reef predators or a proven function in living Gulf sponges.
- **Black sea cucumber, Gulf food and outside-Gulf defense:** [Viyakarn et al. 2020](https://hero.epa.gov/reference/7885235/) report chlorophyll in *H. atra* guts and sediment feeding effects on upper-Gulf reefs. [NOAA American Samoa guidance](https://sanctuaries.noaa.gov/about/pdfs/seacucumber.pdf) describes expulsion of internal organs when attacked and identifies the black, often sand-dusted form. These are not measurements of tank behavior. This species lacks Cuvierian tubules; do not depict those as its defense.
- **Nudibranch defense, other region:** [Ritson-Williams and Paul 2007](https://repository.si.edu/bitstreams/4ee3de11-04c8-4bce-93c9-8588570c6d68/download) tested crude extracts from *P. varicosa* collected in Palau, mixed into artificial food offered to reef fish at Guam. Fish ate less of the extract-containing food than the control; this assay does not establish the response of a Gulf boxfish to a live nudibranch.
- **Christmas tree worm feeding, different species:** [University of the West Indies, *Spirobranchus giganteus* profile](https://sta.uwi.edu/fst/lifesciences/sites/default/files/lifesciences/documents/ogatt/Spirobranchus_giganteus%20-%20Christmas%20Tree%20Worm.pdf) describes filtration of plankton and detritus. Its Caribbean subject is a related worm, not a direct diet measurement of the Koh Tao individual.
- **Barrel sponge nutrition, other region:** [Wooster et al., Red Sea field study](https://repository.kaust.edu.sa/bitstreams/99febf76-c8b5-41c3-a119-3687ad31e57f/download) compared the incoming and outgoing water around *X. testudinaria*. Across their sites the measured carbon uptake consisted mainly of dissolved organic matter and detritus; this does not set the proportions for the Gulf.
- **Banded shrimp shelter, other region:** [Chen and Huang 2012](https://libknowledge.nmns.edu.tw/nmns/upload/bulletin/000000202/209000c/201212-41.pdf), p. 43, describes shrimp waiting in crevices or at shelter exits at a southern Taiwan cleaning station; the observation does not specify time of day. The behavior does not prove that every visit to a crevice is a response to a predator.
- **Scroll coral feeding, different species:** [Red Sea laboratory study of *Turbinaria reniformis*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9600528/) traces nitrogen from fed prey. The Gulf coral is identified only to genus, so the experiment is an example from another species, not evidence of a specific Gulf species or diet.
- **Table coral nutrition, other region:** [NOAA's American Samoa coral-reef food table](https://sanctuaries.noaa.gov/media/docs/20231129-coral-reef-organisms-table.pdf) lists plankton and photosymbiont carbohydrates for *Acropora hyacinthus*. The poster's genus-level table coral does not establish which *Acropora* species is depicted.
- **Leather coral feeding, other region:** [Aksari et al. 2020](https://ojs.unimal.ac.id/index.php/acta-aquatica/article/view/2526) report feeding rates of cultured *Sarcophyton* sp. offered *Artemia* nauplii in aquarium experiments in Bali. This does not establish the food composition of Gulf colonies.
- **Massive *Porites* mucus, other region:** [Bessell-Browne et al. 2017](https://wamsi.org.au/wp-content/uploads/bsk-pdf-manager/2019/10/Dredging-Science-Final-Report-PROJ-4.8-Mucous-sheet-production-in-Porites-Bessell-Browne-et-al-April-2017.pdf) reports mucus-sheet formation and shedding, leaving cleaner tissue, for Western Australian massive *P. lutea*/*P. lobata* corals grouped as *Porites* spp.; the species are difficult to distinguish in that survey. This is sediment removal, not demonstrated anti-predator defense.
- **Acropora nematocysts, different species:** [Hemond et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4320547/) studied gene expression in *A. cervicornis* and *A. palmata* from Panama and describes nematocysts as serving prey capture and defense. It does not directly test predator defense in the Gulf table coral.
- **Flowerpot coral food, different species:** [Ding et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34827743/) tested feed treatments including microalgae and yeast on cultured *Goniopora columna* in Taiwan. The Gulf model has not been identified to species; this is an example from another species, not a claim of its wild Gulf diet.
- **Scroll coral mucus, other region:** [Jones et al. 2019](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0216248) observed some *Turbinaria* spp. using mucus in Western Australian sediment exposure. The source does not assign this observation to the unconfirmed Gulf species or establish anti-predator defense.
- **Leather coral deterrence, other region:** [Hoang et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1134/S1063074022020055) tested pellets containing extracts and sclerites from five Vietnamese *Sarcophyton* taxa against reef fishes and captive moon wrasses; extracts from four of the five reduced feeding in field and aquarium trials. The Gulf *Sarcophyton* species is unconfirmed, so do not imply all of its possible predators are deterred.
- **Cup coral nematocysts, other region:** [Kitahara et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32128117/) isolated nematocysts from Brazilian *T. coccinea* and identified protein sequences homologous to known toxins. Their specific ecological role in Gulf animals was not tested.
- **Hermit shell protection, group-level:** [Gravel et al. 2004](https://www.sekj.org/PDF/anz41-free/anz41-477.pdf) describe hermit crabs' reliance on gastropod shells for protection and experimentally test shell choice in *Pagurus longicarpus*. This is general hermit-crab biology, not a predator trial on Gulf *Dardanus lagopodes*.


### Accessible source alternatives used in the review poster

- [East Asian Seas atlas, Gulf entry](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcovop/2023331201/2023331201.pdf) describes the semi-enclosed Gulf and its approximately 45 m mean depth.
- [Thai DMCR bleaching report](https://www.dmcr.go.th/detailLib/651) describes the 1998 event as particularly severe in the Gulf. The 2024 claim continues to cite the separate Ko Ngam Noi paper.
- [Bessell-Browne 2017 thesis, Chapter 3](https://research-repository.uwa.edu.au/files/21429380/THESIS_DOCTOR_OF_PHILOSOPHY_BESSELL_BROWNE_Pia_2017.pdf) reproduces the sediment and mucus study; the separate [2019 smothering study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0216248) reports this observation too. The biological function remains a research inference, not a predator-defense demonstration.
- [Sangmanee et al. abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32753190/) identifies the same Okinawan Porites feeding experiment; an [author-repository copy](https://shizuoka.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/12704/files/220701001.pdf) supplies the full manuscript.

- [Smithsonian’s striped hermit crab account](https://nationalzoo.si.edu/animals/striped-hermit-crab) describes gastropod-shell protection in that species and many other hermit crabs. It is a general comparison, not a Gulf experiment. [Hoang et al. 2022 abstract at FAO AGRIS](https://agris.fao.org/search/ar/records/65df50b96eef00c2cea26a09) describes the Vietnamese soft-coral extract feeding trials; its full publisher text was inaccessible during this review.

- [Sutthacheep 2022, Koh Tao pinnacles](https://ph02.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/RIST/article/view/247959) lists yellow boxfish at Hin Khao. The earlier inner-Gulf checklist remains independent evidence but is no longer needed for the poster occurrence sentence.
- [Western Gulf coral survey, 2019](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1312/7/11/408), benthic community table, records *Tubastraea coccinea*. The indexed table is readable; live access returned HTTP 429 during this check. The Thai coral checklist remains a second record.

- [Mehrotra et al. 2024](https://repository.naturalis.nl/pub/800405/Mehrotra-2024-Parasites-or-predators-A.pdf), Table 1, directly reports 160 colonies of *Tubastraea coccinea* at Koh Tao. This accessible primary paper replaces the poster link to the western-Gulf paper, whose live site returned HTTP 429.
