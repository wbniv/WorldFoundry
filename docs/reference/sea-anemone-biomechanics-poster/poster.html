LIVING TISSUE · ADHESIVE BASE · MOVING WATER

Sea anemones

Slow journeys. Flexible tentacles. A fluid-supported body.

Soft tentacles, not spines

Muscular tissue bends, shortens and extends against fluid pressure. Stinging cells are microscopic; the visible crown is soft. [6]

Our focus: Entacmaea quadricolor, the bubble-tip. Dense, uneven tentacles can mix swollen and slender tips. The foot is usually hidden in a rock crevice. [1]

BIOLOGY FIRST · SCHEMATICS BELOW

01 · A foot that can relocate

Adults can move to new positions in an aquarium. Daily observations confirm relocation, but do not give a crawling speed. [2]

Foot adhesion is studied in Exaiptasia. [4] This moving-attachment sequence is a visual approximation, pending bubble-tip footage.

02 · Flow gives a shared rhythm

Field observations of Aiptasia diaphana show downstream bending and wave-linked flutter; long tentacles make larger excursions. [3]

For sheltered bubble-tips: gentle coherent lean, curved tips and local lag. This transfer is a modeling inference.

03 · Muscles change the shape

Layer active shortening and recovery onto water motion. Roots remain attached to the disc; tips respond more visibly. [6]

Stomphia can detach and swim after particular predator stimuli. [5] That is a separate species' behavior, not the bubble-tip default.

04 · A continuous fleshy surface

Use curved tube geometry, blunt tips, uneven bulbs and a dense crown. Striation, speckles and translucency are documented in bubble-tips. [1]

256² texture atlas is a proposed game choice. Fine markings belong in the texture; silhouette and bending need geometry.
