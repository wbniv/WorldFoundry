# Rainbow goby research — 6 October 2026

The freshwater Planted Tank resident will represent an adult male **Stiphodon
ornatus**, rather than a generic fish sold under the ambiguous “rainbow goby”
trade name. This is a planted aquarium interpretation of a stream fish.

## Evidence and modelling consequences

Maeda and Tan's specimen-based [2013 review](https://www.science.nus.edu.sg/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2024/07/61rbz749-761.pdf)
documents *S. ornatus* from western Sumatra and illustrates males and females.
Its male diagnosis includes an elongated first dorsal, a large caudal fin,
joined pelvic fins and patterned pectoral rays. Model an elongated low body,
high eyes, two distinct dorsals and a broad tail; do not recycle the betta shape.

The [species profile](https://www.seriouslyfish.com/species/stiphodon-ornatus/)
describes benthic biofilm grazing and the pelvic disc's role in holding position
and providing feeding leverage. That supports a perch → short swim → settle →
graze loop, with the body nearly still while attached and small head movements.
The proposed controller speeds, attachment distance and animation timing are
game choices, not measured biological constants.

The profile warns that several fish may share this trade name. Colour can vary
with mood; choose one consistent blue/turquoise male with orange dorsal rays,
instead of claiming that a literal rainbow gradient is species coloration.
The profile quotes 70 mm standard length; standard length excludes the tail.
Use the primary review's images and proportions for the shape, rather than
treating that maximum as the intended model size.

Stream habitat is principally rock and biofilm; dense rooted planting is an
aquarium setting. Provide visible rounded grazing stones among the plants.
Do not claim that the dense planted tank recreates the species' wild habitat.
Adult freshwater residence is compatible with an amphidromous life history;
there is no breeding or marine larval simulation in this feature.

## Scope and uncertainties

First implementation attaches to authored stone tops and the substrate. It
does not simulate a pressure field or waterfall climbing. Glass attachment and
arbitrary surface normals are later work. Resting pectoral motion, restrained
tail waves and grazing nods convey the difference from an urchin without adding
separate actors for each ray or fin. Review movement on Chromecast; no desktop
timing claims will be used to evaluate performance.

## Texture revision — 6 October

The [Green Aqua live-fish photograph](https://greenaqua.hu/en/stiphodon-sp.html)
was inspected as a colour reference, not copied into the texture. It shows a
silver/brown scaled body with blue on the cheek, orange dorsals and patterned
fin rays. The primary review describes posterior male bars and spotted pectoral
rays in preserved specimens; preserved colours do not establish live hues.
The revised authored albedo therefore uses staggered scale crescents, broken
posterior bars, a local turquoise operculum, orange first-dorsal membrane with
dark rays, spotted second dorsal and caudal/pectoral rays, and a restrained
cyan fin margin. Live specimens vary; this is one chosen appearance.
