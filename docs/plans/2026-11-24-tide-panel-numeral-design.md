## What is happening

The high and low rows use separate rotating digit drums and a dark plate for each changing digit. The minute units digit is a fixed zero with no matching plate. The Arabic outlines are generated from Blender's default font; each glyph is independently reduced to fit the original narrow digit window, so wide and narrow digits can end up at different scales. Colons, decimal points and Thai unit labels are separate geometry. Together these choices produce a patchwork appearance. Confirm the contribution of each in matched renders before choosing the final treatment; the capture alone does not establish every cause.

The recent change to Arabic digits applies to the **side panel**. Preserve the Thai words and units and the clock tower's existing dial options. Keep the existing tide calculations, rounding, high/low ordering and settings behavior. The phone joystick is already being removed in separate work; this plan does not include or require verifying that change.

## Design and implementation

1. Capture a reproducible baseline at a fixed time, including both rows and examples containing `0`, `1`, `3` and `8`. Inspect all ten glyphs, including the fixed zero, in the actual renderer. Record the source and asset hashes and the framing used.
2. Choose one explicit, consistently weighted numeral font with suitable licensing and tabular spacing, or adjust the geometry to produce the same effect. Use a shared scale and baseline across digits; provide enough width for the widest glyph rather than scaling each independently. Make the fixed zero visually match changing digits. Integrate the colon, decimal point and Thai unit into a clear, balanced reading. Consider a continuous backing for each reading instead of isolated plates, and retain the harbour's visual character. Check the full range of digits and values rather than designing around one screenshot.
3. Update the level's panel geometry and glyph generation, keeping the existing drum mechanism where practical. Ensure the digits fit without clipping or overlapping their neighbors, the arrow or the panel edge. Check the panel's existing aspect-aware placement and day/night contrast; preserve the map and tower. Respect the engine-change permission rule: if engine work proves necessary, describe it and obtain explicit permission before editing engine code.
4. Rebuild the standalone Tide level, then the Clocks bundle and APK from the intended assets, taking care not to overwrite concurrent work. Confirm the packaged Tide bytes match the tested standalone and freeze the APK with its hash.

## Verification and acceptance

- Render matched before/after views at the same time, camera and dimensions, plus representative wide and narrow aspect ratios. Show both rows and all digits; check consistent height, baseline, weight, spacing and backing, including the fixed zero and punctuation. Confirm the readings remain legible and fit without clipping or overlap.
- Exercise high and low values, leading zeros, ten-minute rounding, a digit change and a high/low event change. Verify the displayed values against the existing model and check that the drum transitions do not expose neighboring digits or flicker. Run the relevant Tide panel, geometry, Forth and settings tests; add a targeted regression check where it protects the chosen layout.
- Test the frozen combined APK on the Pixel and an available Chromecast through owned coordinator sessions. Capture the panel at the device's actual aspect ratio, inspect its edges, contrast and readability, and check at a normal TV viewing distance where possible. Record the job, device, APK hash, captures and any untested conditions. Do not claim TV readability from a desktop image alone.
- Update the Tide plan with the final design, before/after evidence, tests and device results. Acceptance requires coherent, readable numbers and no panel clipping at the tested screen shapes; record any remaining visual or device limitations explicitly.
