The tide at Bangkok
What the Chao Phraya does every day, how a computer can add it up, and how far to trust the sum. Station: Royal Thai Navy Headquarters, 13° 44′ N 100° 29′ E.

K1 and O1 come once a day, M2 and S2 twice. Added, they make the lumpy curve above. 64 such waves are used in all.

4 How good is it?ours (measured)
Fitted to 2026 and scored on 2027, and the reverse: median error 4.1 to 4.5 cm, 90th percentile 11 cm, on a range of 2.8 m.
5 Which clock does the table use?ours (measured)
Observed hourly water at Ko Lak (GMT) lines up with the Navy’s table only when the table is read as UTC+7: Thai local time.
6 What the harbour does with it
The gauge board, the boats and their ropes, the raft and the hyacinth all follow this one number; the dial on the tower tells the time.
7 What we do not knowother source
The Navy’s own constants are not public. Our fit is to its published predictions. No nodal correction. Discharge of the river is absorbed into the annual wave. Bangkok’s form factor is 1.36 here and 1.06 in a model study, both ‘mixed, mainly semidiurnal’.
