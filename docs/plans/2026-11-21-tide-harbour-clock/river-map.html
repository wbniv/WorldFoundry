Tide Harbour: an inset map of the river   proposal, not built

The map, close up
The river is real: its course from the sea to the city, drawn from OpenStreetMap.

The tower marks this harbour: the Navy's Bangkok station, 50 km up the river from its mouth (measured along that line).

The column is the tide here and now, in metres, with the rising or falling arrow. The same model moves the water.

The staff at the bottom is Bangkok Bar, where the Navy publishes a second table.

Not shown as fact: how long high water takes to travel from the bar to the city. Fitting the bar's table with the same tool would measure it; until then the travelling band is a sketch, off by default.

Actors (proposed, untested): one painted board, a lamp at the tower, the pointer and the arrow.

Where it goes. A map fixed to a corner never moves, and this clock's rule against burn-in (D10) is that nothing sits still. So either the inset drifts with the camera, or the same map is a chart board standing on the pier, inside the world. Both are here: Corner and On the pier.
